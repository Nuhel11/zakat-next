    What is zakat?

    Zakat is one of the five pillars of Islam. Every adult Muslim whose wealth stays at or above a minimum amount, called the nisab, for a full lunar year gives 2.5% of it to people in need.

    The nisab is the value of 87.48 grams of gold or 612.36 grams of silver, so it goes up and down with their prices.

    How it is worked out

    1. Add up everything each person owns that counts for zakat.
    2. Take away debts and bills that are due now.
    3. Compare what is left with the nisab.
    4. If it is at or above the nisab and has been for a full lunar year, zakat is 2.5% of it.
    5. Add up everyone's zakat to get the household total.

    What counts

    Include

    • Cash at home and in the bank
    • Gold and silver
    • Shares, funds and savings schemes
    • Stock you are holding to sell
    • Money you are owed and expect back

    Leave out

    • The home you live in
    • Your car, furniture and clothes
    • Tools and equipment you use for work
    • Debts that are not due this year

    Common questions

    Can one person pay zakat for the whole family?

    Zakat is worked out on each person's own wealth, which is why this calculator checks everyone separately. One person can pay on behalf of others, as long as they agree to it.

    Should I use the gold or silver nisab?

    The silver nisab is much lower, so more people end up paying. Many scholars recommend it because it benefits the poor more, while others use gold. Ask your local scholar which your community follows.

    Do I pay zakat on jewellery I wear?

    Scholars differ. The Hanafi school says yes, while the other main schools generally say jewellery worn regularly is exempt. Gold kept as savings counts either way.

    What about pensions?

    Views differ, often depending on whether you can access the money now. If you are unsure, ask a scholar or a zakat organisation.

    When should I pay?

    When your zakat year is complete. Many people choose Ramadan, and it helps to keep to the same date each year.

    This calculator is a guide. Please check with a scholar if you are unsure.
